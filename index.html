03/2025 - 05/2026

Game Development Instructor

Alchemy Technologies

Taught autistic students Unreal Engine Blueprints, gameplay logic, and mechanics.

Working with Alchemy Technologies on a research & development project, taught autistic children the basics of Unreal Engine. This was done using blueprints (to code logic in Unreal Engine).

The first step was to teach them programming and logic basics, this was done on Scratch. Then the transition to unreal engine was made. Games like Subway Surfers clone, Scooby-Doo top-down game, were the major successful projects the students created.

Every student required individual attention and different teaching techniques to teach. It took time to adapt and work with these challanges, but everything worked out well once the initial challanges were overcome.

A part of my job was to also profile the students based on their interests. An example would be of a student who was good in Unreal, better than the rest in terms of the progress, but was not interested in it at all. So we experimented and based on his love for games and elite reaction time, we dropped him in a Tekken tournament after a month of practice. He performed really well, he beat veterens playing the game for years, but in the end couldn't win the tournament. However, it was a major success in our eyes, given the limited practice time, and more importantly the fact that he loved doing this way more than Unreal Engine.
